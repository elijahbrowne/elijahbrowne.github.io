# Meeting notes: Mock privacy audit debrief (fictional)

Date: spring, last year
Attendees: Dana Whitfield (Privacy Officer), clinic manager, front-desk lead, nursing lead

## What the audit found

- 11 privacy incidents logged in one quarter. None caused known harm. All were preventable.
- Phone calls about family members: 4 incidents
- Printed records left in the shared printer: 3 incidents
- Patient talk overheard from the waiting room: 2 incidents
- Unlocked workstations with a record open: 2 incidents

## Why the current training isn't working

- The annual compliance course is a 40-minute slide deck with a quiz.
- Staff average 92% on the quiz but repeat the same mistakes on the floor.
- Staff say the rules make sense on a slide but the real moment comes fast: a ringing phone, a full printer tray, a coworker mid-sentence.

## Decisions

- Replace the slide deck for front-desk staff with short practice built around the real moments above.
- Training must fit in about 10 minutes between patient waves and work on a phone.
- Goal: cut floor-level incidents by half within two quarters.
- Dana will review record-access logs monthly and share anonymized trends at staff meetings.
- Reporting your own mistake carries no penalty. Dana will repeat this in every training.

## Open questions

- Should the course cover minors and guardians? Decision: not in the first version. Route those calls to a nurse.
