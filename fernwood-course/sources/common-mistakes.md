# Common mistakes on the clinic floor (fictional)

Collected by Dana Whitfield, Privacy Officer, from last spring's mock audit and staff check-ins. Each mistake lists what staff did, why it felt right in the moment, and the rule it breaks.

## Phone calls about another person (Rule 1)

- Shared results with a son or daughter because "she's his mom". Felt kind. Breaks Rule 1: family isn't automatic access.
- Said "I can't tell you if she's a patient" and hung up. Nothing leaked, but the caller got no next step.
- Said "Her results aren't back yet." That confirms she is a patient and had tests.

## Verifying a patient on the phone (Rule 2)

- Read the date of birth aloud and asked "Is that right?" A caller who is not the patient can just say yes.
- Checked name only because the caller "sounded like her".
- Discussed the record after the date of birth didn't match, because the caller said they mixed up the year.

## Printed records (Rule 3)

- Left someone else's charts in the tray because "they'll be back in a minute". Patients pass the printer on the way to the restroom.
- Read through the pages to work out whose they were. Returning them is right; reading the whole chart is not.
- Dropped old printouts in the recycling bin instead of the shred bin.

## Talking about patients (Rule 4)

- Said nothing when two nurses discussed a patient's diagnosis by the waiting room door, because "it's their patient".
- Filed a complaint about the conversation but didn't speak up at the time, so it carried on.
- Called a patient by full name and the reason for their visit across the waiting room.

## Workstations (Rule 5)

- Closed the open record on a coworker's screen but left the computer logged in.
- Left their own workstation unlocked "just to grab the printer".

## Opening records (Rule 6)

- Looked up a neighbor's record "to see if she's okay".
- Checked their own lab results in the clinic system instead of the patient portal.
- Opened a coworker's record to find a phone number for a shift swap.
