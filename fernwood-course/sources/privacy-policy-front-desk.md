# Fernwood Health Clinic: Front Desk Privacy Rules (fictional)

Owner: Dana Whitfield, Privacy Officer
Applies to: front-desk associates, medical assistants, and anyone working the clinic floor
Version: 2.1

Fernwood Health Clinic is a fictional clinic created for a training sample. These rules reflect common US clinic practice for protecting patient information. They are not legal advice.

## Key term

Patient information means anything that identifies a patient and relates to their health or care: names with appointments, test results, diagnoses, charts, and billing records. The law calls this protected health information, or PHI.

## Rule 1: Phone calls about another person

Never share or confirm a patient's information with a caller unless the patient has a signed authorization on file naming that caller.

- Being a spouse, parent, or adult child does not give access on its own.
- Do not confirm that the person is a patient here.
- Give the caller a next step: the patient can call us directly, or sign a release at their next visit.
- Exception: a parent or legal guardian of a minor patient, verified in the chart. Send these calls to a nurse.

Key phrase: Family isn't automatic access.

## Rule 2: Verifying a patient on the phone

Before discussing anything with a caller who says they are the patient, verify two identifiers: full name and date of birth.

- Ask the caller to say them. Do not read them out for the caller to confirm.
- If either does not match, do not discuss the record. Offer a call back to the number on file.

Key phrase: Two identifiers, every time.

## Rule 3: Printed records

Never leave printed patient information where others can see it.

- Collect your own printing right away.
- If you find someone else's printing, read only the cover sheet or header, enough to route it.
- Place it in the locked pickup bin beside the printer and message the team channel that it is there.
- Shred printed records you no longer need. Never use the recycling bin.

Key phrase: Read only what you need to route it.

## Rule 4: Talking about patients

Discuss patients only where others cannot overhear: the nurses' station, an exam room, or the staff office.

- Discussing care with colleagues is part of the job. Doing it near the waiting room is not.
- If you hear a colleague discussing a patient in a public area, speak up in the moment and suggest a private space.
- If it keeps happening, tell the Privacy Officer.
- In the waiting room, call patients by first name only.

Key phrase: Move the talk inside.

## Rule 5: Workstations

Lock your computer every time you step away, even for a moment. Press Windows + L.

- If you see an unlocked workstation with a record open, lock it. Do not close the person's work.
- Tell the owner when they return.

Key phrase: Step away, lock it.

## Rule 6: Opening records

Open a patient's record only when your current task requires it.

- Curiosity is not a work reason. This includes records of neighbors, friends, family, coworkers, and well-known people.
- You may not look up your own record through work systems. Request it through the patient portal like any other patient.
- Every record view is logged with your name and the time. The Privacy Officer reviews these logs each month.

Key phrase: Only the records your job requires.

## Reporting

If you think patient information was shared by mistake, tell the Privacy Officer the same day. Early reports are how we fix problems. You will not be penalized for reporting your own mistake.
