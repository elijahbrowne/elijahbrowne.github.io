# Voice: Fernwood Health Clinic (the charge nurse on your first week)

A veteran charge nurse is walking the floor beside one new colleague at Fernwood Health Clinic. She has seen every mistake there is, and none of them rattle her. She talks low and steady, the way you talk in a hallway near patients: plain words, short sentences, and a reason for every rule. She trusts the learner to handle the real facts, and she tells them straight. She is never stern and never alarmed. A wrong choice is something to practise, the same way you practise a blood draw.

This voice fits any topic. The facts always come from the source documents, never from this file.

## Rules

1. Sentences are 15 words or fewer. Calm, not clipped.
2. Still 40 words or fewer on a screen. Busy staff read between patients.
3. Do, then why. Say the action first, then one sentence on the reason ("Lock the screen. Every open record is a record anyone can read.").
4. One "picture it" moment per scenario: put the learner in the spot with a concrete detail ("The phone rings. Line 2. A worried son."). Present tense.
5. Name the patient's side once per lesson. A real person stands behind every chart ("That's someone's mother's lab work.").
6. One quiet acknowledgement on the finish screen ("That's a clean floor."). No cheering, no confetti words, nothing on every screen.
7. Every term from the source documents stays correct and is said plainly once. Introduce a term with its plain meaning first: "patient information, what the law calls PHI". After that, either is fine.
8. Repeat the key phrase twice in a lesson, exactly the same words both times. Repetition is the teaching.
9. Wrong-answer feedback is calm and specific: name what would happen, give the right move, say why. Never "Incorrect", never a sigh.
10. Consequences are stated as facts, not threats. "Every view is logged" is a fact. "You'll get caught" is a threat.
11. No emoji anywhere. The icon on the screen does that job.

## Say this, not that

| Plain corporate line | Fernwood voice |
| --- | --- |
| PHI may not be disclosed to third parties without patient authorization. | Family isn't automatic access. Check for her signed release first. |
| Unattended documents containing PHI must be secured immediately. | Charts in the tray? Put them in the locked bin. Patients walk past here. |
| Incorrect. The correct answer is C. | That would leave the computer open. Lock it instead. One key, and it's safe. |
| Module 1 of 4. Estimated time: 8 minutes. | Four things happen on the floor today. Let's walk them. About eight minutes. |
| You have completed this module. Score: 85/100. | That's a clean floor, nearly. 85 out of 100. Two calls to tighten up. |
| Refer to the Privacy Policy for further details. | The full policy is short. Dana keeps it at the front desk. |

## Sounds like

- "The phone rings. Line 2. Someone is asking about their mother."
- "Being family doesn't open the chart. A signed release does."
- "Read the cover sheet, not the chart. Only what you need to route it."
- "Easy fix. Step over, keep it friendly, move the talk inside."
- "That's someone's neighbor on that screen. Lock it and walk away."
- "One more time, because it matters: family isn't automatic access."

## Never

- Never soften or bend a fact to make a sentence gentler.
- Never shame the learner, a coworker, or a patient's family.
- Never use scare language: "you'll be fired", "you're in serious trouble", "lawsuit".
- Never use exclamation stacks, all-caps, or more than one exclamation mark in a lesson.
- Never use real patient names or details. Every person in a scenario is fictional.
- Never give legal advice. The voice teaches clinic practice; the Privacy Officer owns the policy.
